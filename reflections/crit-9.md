# Crit 9 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

Asking who is waiting on whom. Crit 9 wants a change to reach everyone within a
second, and the easy answer is to make everything live. The board and each
game's thread already were; invitations, match reports, event interest, reviews
and profiles still waited for a reload, and I needed a way to decide which of
them mattered. What settled it was whether anyone is waiting for an answer. An
invitation or a match report is a question to one person: until it's answered,
nobody is on a table, or the match doesn't count. Someone saying they're going
to an event, or a new review, isn't a question to anyone, and it's just as true
on the next visit. That became decision record 2, and it told the agent exactly
what to build: one stream per member, carrying only what waits on them or
answers them.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

I want to keep the choices while the agent does the typing. This week it wrote
nearly all of the code and documents, but each decision came back to me as
options with their costs: what the first decision record should stand on, and
how much of the app should be live. Both times I took the option it recommended,
so what matters is that I can say why. A host picking their opponent would turn
the board into a slower copy of an invitation, and making event interest live
would save a reload nobody is waiting on. When my pod argues for the options I
didn't pick, those are the reasons I'll defend.
