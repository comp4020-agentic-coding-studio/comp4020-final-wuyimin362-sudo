# Crit 8 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

Cutting the app down to one interaction. My first plan for the club was a long
list: player profiles with each person's blade and rubbers, ratings, opponents'
reviews, a six-sided strength chart, private messages, nearby players, and
sign-ups for official tournaments. When the agent checked that list against the
brief, most of it turned out to be what other apps already do. I cut it back to
posting a game and another member taking it up, and that made the first version
possible: two tables, one check a stranger could pass, and an app live the same
night.

Once that worked, I brought the rest of my list back, but not as it was. Each
feature came back with a limit aimed at the problem the check had found: scores
count only once the opponent confirms them, reviews come only from real
opponents, messages live inside a game, and the app links to Table Tennis ACT
instead of taking entries. Cutting first is what let me add things back on
purpose.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

I want to build small tools for the communities I'm actually part of. I play at
an ACT club, and the people who will use this board are people I play with. Most
of my first ideas were features big platforms have. What makes them fit a club
is where their limits sit: nothing a member shares is visible outside the club,
and the app leaves ratings and entries to Table Tennis ACT. Building for people
I know, rather than for scale, is what I want to keep doing.
