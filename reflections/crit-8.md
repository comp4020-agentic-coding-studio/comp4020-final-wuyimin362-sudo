# Crit 8 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

Cutting the app down to one interaction. My first plan for the club was a long
list: player profiles with each person's blade and rubbers, ratings, opponents'
reviews, a six-sided strength chart, private messages, nearby players, and
sign-ups for official tournaments. When the agent checked that list against the
brief, most of it turned out to be what other apps already do, and private
messages were close to the chat room the brief warns about. I kept the club and
cut everything else back to posting a game and another member taking it up.

That one decision made the rest of the evening possible. The schema became two
tables, the crit 8 check became one journey a stranger could take, and the app
was live the same night. The features I dropped didn't just disappear: they
became the README's list of things I chose not to build, each with a reason.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

I want to build small tools for the communities I'm actually part of. I play at
an ACT club, and the people who will use this board are people I play with. Most
of my first ideas were features that big platforms have. A club doesn't need
them: its members already know each other, and Table Tennis ACT already keeps
the ratings. What the club is missing is simpler, a way to find someone who's
free for a game. Building for people I know means I can leave everything else
out.
